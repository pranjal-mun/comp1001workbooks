How bosses work

A boss problem is bigger than the practice questions in the workbook, but it only uses what Lectures 4 to 11 covered: input() with int() and float(), arithmetic including %, string indexes and slices, f-strings, if/elif/else with nesting, the Boolean operators and, or and not, and string tests such as isdigit() and isupper(). No loops, no functions, and no import. Each problem lists the rules its checker enforces. A program that prints the right answer but breaks a rule does not pass. Term tests and the final exam have questions of this kind, and you write the code in PyLab there as well.

Every problem here reads its values with input(). Keep those lines exactly as they are: Check runs your program several times, typing different answers at the prompts each time, and every run must print exactly what the table shows. Defeat a boss and its marker on the workbooks page is crossed off.

Press ▶ Run to try your program and Check to fight the boss. The bosses get harder from 👹 to 💀 and are worth 10, 15, 20, and 25 XP.

👹 Boss 1: Leap Year

Most years have 365 days. A leap year has 366, and the rule for spotting one has three parts. A year divisible by 4 is a leap year, except that a year divisible by 100 is not, unless it is also divisible by 400. So 2024 is a leap year, 1900 is not, and 2000 is. You are given a year, year. Say whether it is a leap year.

Read year with input() and print one sentence.

Example 1
Input: year = 2024
Output: 2024 is a leap year.
Example 2
Input: year = 1900
Output: 1900 is not a leap year.
Explanation: 1900 is divisible by 4 and by 100, but not by 400.
Constraints
  • 1 <= year <= 9999

The program reads the year after the prompt Year: .

Year: 2024
2024 is a leap year.
TypedOutput
20242024 is a leap year.
20232023 is not a leap year.
19001900 is not a leap year.
20002000 is a leap year.
21002100 is not a leap year.
16001600 is a leap year.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • One condition that joins the three % tests with and and or.
  • Print one line.

Keep the line year = int(input('Year: ')) unchanged. The checker will type the other years.

👾 Boss 2: Licence Plate

A province is changing the shape of its licence plates. An old style plate is three capital letters followed by three digits, like ABC123. A new style plate is four digits followed by three capital letters, like 1234ABC. You are given a plate, plate. Say whether it is old style, new style, or not a valid plate.

Read plate with input() and print one sentence.

Example 1
Input: plate = 1234ABC
Output: 1234ABC is a new style plate.
Example 2
Input: plate = abc123
Output: abc123 is not a valid plate.
Explanation: The letters must be capitals.
Constraints
  • 0 <= len(plate) <= 10
  • plate has no spaces.

Plate: ABC123
ABC123 is an old style plate.

  • One if/elif/else chain: old style, new style, not valid.
  • Test the length with len(), then the parts with slices and isalpha(), isupper() and isdigit(), joined with and.
  • Print one line.

Keep the line plate = input('Plate: ') unchanged. The checker will type the other plates.

🐉 Boss 3: Roulette Payout

An American roulette wheel has 38 pockets: 1 to 36, 0 and 00. Players bet on a single number, on a colour, on odd or even, or on a half of the wheel. The colours follow a pattern: from 1 to 10 and 19 to 28 the odd numbers are red and the even numbers black, and from 11 to 18 and 29 to 36 it is the other way round. 0 and 00 are green and pay nothing except the number itself. You are given the pocket the ball landed in, spin. Print every bet that pays.

Explanation: 13 is in the 11 to 18 group, where odd numbers are black.

Constraints

💀 Boss 4: Next Day

You are given a date as three whole numbers: year, month and day. Print the date of the following day. Most of the time only the day changes, but the last day of a month rolls into the next month, and December 31 rolls into the next year. February has 28 days, or 29 in a leap year, using the rule from Boss 1. April, June, September and November have 30 days. The rest have 31.

Read the three values with input() and print one sentence with both dates in the form YYYY-MM-DD, so month and day always have two digits.

Example 1
Input: year = 2013, month = 11, day = 18
Output: The day after 2013-11-18 is 2013-11-19.
Example 2
Input: year = 2013, month = 12, day = 31
Output: The day after 2013-12-31 is 2014-01-01.
Explanation: The day rolls into a new month, and the month rolls into a new year.
Example 3
Input: year = 2100, month = 2, day = 28
Output: The day after 2100-02-28 is 2100-03-01.
Explanation: 2100 is divisible by 100 but not by 400, so February has 28 days.
Constraints
  • 1 <= year <= 9998, 1 <= month <= 12
  • The date is always valid.
