How bosses work

A boss problem is bigger than the practice questions in the workbook, but it only uses what Lectures 4 to 8 covered: input() with int() and float(), arithmetic including %, string indexes and .find(), f-strings, and one two-way decision with if and else. Plan the steps on paper first, then write the program. Each problem lists the rules its checker enforces. A program that prints the right answer but breaks a rule does not pass. Term tests and the final exam have questions of this kind, and you write the code in PyLab there as well.

Every problem here reads its value with input(). Keep that line exactly as it is: Check runs your program several times, typing a different answer at the prompt each time, and every run must print exactly what the table shows. Defeat a boss and its marker on the workbooks page is crossed off.

Press ▶ Run to try your program and Check to fight the boss. The bosses get harder from 👹 to 🐉 and are worth 10, 15, and 20 XP.

👹 Boss 1: Even or Odd

You are given an integer, number. Print one sentence, ending with a full stop, that says whether it is even or odd.

Read number with input(), choose the word inside the branches, and print the sentence once after them.

Example 1
Input: number = 14
Output: 14 is even.
Example 2
Input: number = -3
Output: -3 is odd.
Explanation: 0 is even, and negative numbers can be even or odd too.
Constraints
  • -1000 <= number <= 1000

The program reads the number after the prompt Number: .

Number: 14
14 is even.
TypedOutput
1414 is even.
77 is odd.
00 is even.
-3-3 is odd.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • Use % to test the remainder.
  • One print(), after the branches.
  • End the sentence with a full stop.

Keep the line number = int(input('Number: ')) unchanged. The checker will type the other numbers.

👾 Boss 2: Dog Years

A common rule says one human year equals seven dog years. A better rule counts each of a dog's first two years as 10.5 dog years and every year after that as 4 dog years. You are given a dog's age in human years, years, as a float. Print both ages to one decimal place.

Read years with input(), work out the dog years inside the branches, and print once after them.

Example 1
Input: years = 7
Output: 7.0 human years is 41.0 dog years.
Explanation: Oliver is 7. His first two years count 2 * 10.5 = 21 and the other five count 5 * 4 = 20.
Example 2
Input: years = 1.2
Output: 1.2 human years is 12.6 dog years.
Explanation: 1.2 is within the first two years, so 1.2 * 10.5 = 12.6.
Example 3
Input: years = 2
Output: 2.0 human years is 21.0 dog years.
Explanation: A 2-year-old dog is 21 in dog years under either rate.
Constraints
  • 0 <= years <= 30
  • years may have a decimal part.

The program reads the age after the prompt Human years: .

Human years: 7
7.0 human years is 41.0 dog years.
TypedOutput
77.0 human years is 41.0 dog years.
22.0 human years is 21.0 dog years.
11.0 human years is 10.5 dog years.
1515.0 human years is 73.0 dog years.
1.21.2 human years is 12.6 dog years.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • Use the rates 10.5 and 4 in the code.
  • Show both numbers with :.1f.
  • One print(), after the branches.

Keep the line years = float(input('Human years: ')) unchanged. The checker will type the other ages.

🐉 Boss 3: Colour of a Chess Square

A chess board names each square with a column letter from a to h and a row number from 1 to 8. The corner square a1 is black, and the colours alternate along every row and every column, so b1 is white, a2 is white, and b2 is black again. You are given a square name, position. Print its colour.

Read position with input() and print one line. Do not write one if per letter: turn the letter into a number with 'abcdefgh'.find(position[0]), which is 0 for a, 1 for b, and so on up to 7.

Example 1
Input: position = d5
Output: d5 is a white square.
Example 2
Input: position = a1
Output: a1 is a black square.
Example 3
Input: position = h1
Output: h1 is a white square.
Explanation: h1 is seven squares to the right of a1, an odd number of colour changes.
Constraints
  • position is two characters: a lowercase letter from a to h, then a digit from 1 to 8.
  • The square is always valid.

The program reads the square after the prompt Square: .

Square: d5
d5 is a white square.
TypedOutput
a1a1 is a black square.
d5d5 is a white square.
h1h1 is a white square.
g7g7 is a black square.
c6c6 is a white square.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Pick the letter and the digit out of position with indexes.
  • Turn the letter into a number with .find(). No ord().
  • Use % to decide the colour.
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • One print(), after the branches.

Keep the line position = input('Square: ') unchanged. The checker will type the other squares.
