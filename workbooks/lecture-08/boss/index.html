How bosses work

A boss problem is bigger than the practice questions in the workbook, but it only uses what Lectures 4 to 8 covered: input() with int() and float(), arithmetic including %, string indexes and .find(), f-strings, and one two-way decision with if and else. There are no hints in the code box. Plan the steps on paper first, then write the program. Each problem lists the rules its checker enforces. A program that prints the right answer but breaks a rule does not pass. Term tests and the final exam have questions of this kind, and you write the code in PyLab there as well.

Every problem here reads its value with input(). Keep that line exactly as it is: Check runs your program several times, typing a different answer at the prompt each time, and every run must print exactly what the table shows. Defeat a boss and its marker on the workbooks page is crossed off.

Press ▶ Run to try your program and Check to fight the boss. The bosses get harder from 👹 to 🐉 and are worth 10, 15, and 20 XP.

👹 Boss 1: Even or Odd

Read an integer from the keyboard and report whether it is even or odd, in one sentence that ends with a full stop:

Number: 14
14 is even.

An even number leaves a remainder of 0 when divided by 2. Test number % 2. This also works for 0 and for negative numbers. Choose the word inside the branches and print the sentence once after them.

TypedOutput
1414 is even.
77 is odd.
00 is even.
-3-3 is odd.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • Use % to test the remainder.
  • One print(), after the branches.
  • End the sentence with a full stop.

Keep the line number = int(input('Number: ')) unchanged. The checker will type the other numbers.

👾 Boss 2: Dog Years

A common rule says one human year equals seven dog years. A better rule counts each of a dog's first two years as 10.5 dog years and every year after that as 4 dog years. Oliver is 7. The first two years count 21 dog years and the remaining five years count 20, which makes 41.

Read the dog's age in human years as a float and print both ages to one decimal place:

Human years: 7
7.0 human years is 41.0 dog years.

The decision is whether the dog is still in its first two years. Ages of 2 or less use the 10.5 rate on the whole age. Older dogs get 21 for the first two years plus 4 for each year beyond them. Work out the dog years inside the branches, then print once. A 2-year-old dog is 21 in dog years in either branch. Check that your program prints 21.0 for 2.

TypedOutput
77.0 human years is 41.0 dog years.
22.0 human years is 21.0 dog years.
11.0 human years is 10.5 dog years.
1515.0 human years is 73.0 dog years.
1.21.2 human years is 12.6 dog years.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • Use the rates 10.5 and 4 in the code.
  • Show both numbers with :.1f.
  • One print(), after the branches.

Keep the line years = float(input('Human years: ')) unchanged. The checker will type the other ages.

🐉 Boss 3: Colour of a Chess Square

A chess board names each square with a column letter from a to h and a row number from 1 to 8. The square a1 in the corner is black, and the colours alternate along every row and every column, so b1 is white, a2 is white, and b2 is black again.

Read a square name such as d5 and print its colour:

Square: d5
d5 is a white square.

Do not write one if per letter. Turn the letter into a number instead: 'abcdefgh'.find(position[0]) is 0 for a, 1 for b, and so on up to 7. Turn the digit position[1] into an int. Moving one square in any direction changes the sum by one. The sum is odd on every black square and even on every white square. For a1 it is 0 + 1. You may assume the square typed is valid.

TypedOutput
a1a1 is a black square.
d5d5 is a white square.
h1h1 is a white square.
g7g7 is a black square.
c6c6 is a white square.
Rules the checker enforces
  • Pick the letter and the digit out of position with indexes.
  • Turn the letter into a number with .find(). No ord().
  • Use % to decide the colour.
  • Exactly one if with an else. No elif.
  • One print(), after the branches.

Keep the line position = input('Square: ') unchanged. The checker will type the other squares.
